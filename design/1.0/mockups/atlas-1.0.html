Why does the worm algorithm’s autocorrelation time grow so fast near the transition, and is our mixing test measuring the right thing?

Your mixing test measures the integrated autocorrelation time of the energy, but the slow mode near the transition is the winding number. For the worm update the paper beside reports

τW ∝ Lz,  z ≈ 0.25,

while the energy decorrelates almost at once, so a test on E alone calls the chain mixed long before W is. I would add the winding-number autocorrelation to notes/mixing-test.md and rerun at L = 32 and 64.

Worm algorithms for classical statistical models

A short invented excerpt for the mockup

The worm update works in the space of closed and open paths. Its moves are local, yet they change topological sectors as easily as any other configuration, which removes the critical slowing-down of conventional local updates for most observables.

G(i, j) = Z−1 Σworms δI,i δM,j W(C)

For the energy and the susceptibility we find dynamical exponents compatible with zero. The winding-number autocorrelation time grows as Lz with z ≈ 0.25, the slowest mode we observed; any convergence test should include it.

Table II lists the integrated autocorrelation times for lattices from L = 8 to 128, measured in sweeps of the worm head.
